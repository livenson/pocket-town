# Review: simplicity, ease of use and stability (November 2026)

Asked by the owner: review Pocket Town for simplicity, ease of use and stability; plan first, then check against real navigation. Later requests in the same session: check on a phone view, the doors, the map houses, the house wizard and editor, the furniture, and a hidden piano for grown-ups.

## Plan

| # | Area | How it was checked |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | First run | Fresh device to first friend fed: taps, seconds, anything a pre-reader must read |
| U2 | Way-finding | Every screen: back and home, number of controls, dead ends |
| U3 | Core loop | Give food by tap-tap and drag, bot card, nudge, follow, undo and redo |
| U4 | Creation | Avatar and house wizards, decorating in place |
| U5 | Touch and labels | Every visible control: at least 44 px, has an accessible name |
| S1 | Clean runtime | Page errors, console errors, failed or third-party requests on every journey |
| S2 | Monkey | 400 random taps on visible controls; the screen must never go blank or throw |
| S3 | Saves | Reload mid-play and while carrying, both save slots corrupted, "while you were away" |
| S4 | Leaks | 120 visits scene, map, building and door, with forced garbage collection |
| S5 | Layout churn | Rotate and resize while playing; a minute of fast-forward |
| S6 | Offline | Service worker on, network cut, reload, play on |

## Method

- The production build (`npm run build && npm run preview`), driven by Playwright scripts that tap like a child: phone 390 × 844 and tablet 1180 × 820 with touch, desktop 1440 × 900 with a mouse. Every step was screenshotted and looked at.
- The navigation scripts ran in Chromium (the only browser installed here), with touch emulation for phone and tablet. The full e2e suite then ran in the CI image, where phone and tablet use WebKit, Safari's engine. A real iPad is still the final test.
- Baseline before changes: 124 unit tests, lint and build all passed.

## What works well

- **First run is fast.** From opening the link to feeding a friend: 7 taps on tablet and desktop, 8 on phone, about 8 seconds. The goal is under 60 seconds.
- **No runtime errors anywhere.** No page errors or console errors and no third-party requests in any run, including 400 random taps on a phone, resize and rotate churn, and a minute of fast-forward.
- **No memory leak.** The JS heap settles at 22–27 MB after 120 room visits with forced garbage collection; DOM nodes and listeners stay flat; one canvas throughout.
- **Plays offline.** With the service worker installed and the network cut, a reload opens the town, the map and the rooms.
- **Saves hold up.** Reloading mid-play, and while carrying something, lands back safely. With both save slots corrupted, the game still opens (a fresh town, no error). The "while you were away" postcard shows after a faked three-hour gap.
- **Back and home** exist on every screen after onboarding. Nothing dead-ends.
- **Room editor**: tapping a piece, then the room, is clear, and the drawer shows real pictures.

## Fixed in this pass

Ranked by how much they got in a child's way.

1. **Phone: half of every room was unreachable.** In portrait the room is wider than the screen and the camera only moved with a selected character, so doors, the sofa and anything near the edges could not be seen or tapped. Now arrows at the stage edges look left and right. They appear only when there is more room to see, and tapping a character hands the camera back.
2. **Phone: moving in was blocked.** In the house wizard's move-in step, the front door (the place the child is told to tap) sat under the residents panel. Now a compact house and four residents per row keep everything on one screen.
3. **Front and inner doors looked identical.** Both were the same brown arch, and only the hidden label differed. The front door now has a round sky window and a doormat (teal unless painted).
4. **House wizard furniture was abstract.** It showed icons on coloured tiles (the bench was a "minus") while the room shows drawings, so the same sofa looked like two things. The wizard now uses the room's drawings. The 28 px "try it with a character" checkbox is now a full-size switch.
5. **Map houses looked unfinished.** A leftover CSS rule put a white card with a red top behind every building, and every place was the same box with an icon. Buildings are now drawn: cottage, tall house, round house (a built house keeps its shape), café, library, music hall and park gate, each with a small place sign. Character pins sit above the roofs.
6. **Furniture style was inconsistent.** Some pieces had ink outlines and most had none, and the music box was an unreadable dark blob. All 51 drawn pieces were redrawn to one standard with outlines, a highlight and a shade. Every id, size, feet line and surface height is kept, so saves and the simulation are unaffected.
7. **Tablet portrait: the drawer's page dots ran over the category tabs.** This was already in the reference picture. A narrow drawer now hides the dots; the arrows still page.
8. **New: a hidden piano for grown-ups.** Three quick taps on the version line in the grown-up area open it. Six instruments, chords, sliding along the keys, and letter keys on a computer. All synthesised, no files.

## Still open (recommendations)

### Simplicity and ease of use

- **Phone carry mode is crowded.** While carrying something, every valid target shows a dashed outline and a tick, and they overlap around characters and the table. The carry banner covers the shelf target. Floor spots and surfaces are 37–41 px tall on phone, below the 44 px rule. Suggest: on phone, show ticks only on characters and one floor spot, and make the surface bands at least 44 px.
- **Phone object drawer pages deeply.** Three things per page means ten pages of food. The page dots are 35 px wide. Suggest two rows of three on phone, and dots as indicators rather than buttons (the arrows already page).
- **The map has no "back".** The only button is Home, which goes to the hub, not the room the child came from. A house icon also means "hub" here, while "My house" is a building. Suggest a back arrow to the last room; keep Home for the hub.
- **Hub on phone:** the four big tiles fall below the fold, and the locked "Friends" tile takes a quarter of them. Suggest hiding Friends until it exists, so the other three fit.
- **Two ways to place furniture.** The house wizard's 12-cell grid and the in-room editor do the same job differently. Suggest that the wizard end after rooms and paint ("go in and decorate"), so there is one way to furnish. That would also shorten the wizard from eight steps.
- **Busy play screen on tablet:** about 43 controls at once (top bar, character dock, drawer with six tabs, time, camera, hint). Workable, but the most for a 6-year-old to scan. The decorate drawer has ten category tabs.
- **Wizard step rail on phone** leaves an empty band under the icons.

### Stability

- **No WebGL means a blank stage (from reading the code; not reproduced).** If WebGL fails to start, `getStage()` rejects once, the failed promise is kept, and the stage stays empty with no retry or message. Suggest catching it: retry once, otherwise show the "peg fixing things" state the plan describes.
- **WebGL context loss on iPad is untested.** Safari drops WebGL contexts under memory pressure and in the background. Worth a real-iPad check: leave the app in the background for a while, come back, and see whether the room redraws.
- **A bad world reaches both save slots within a minute.** Both slots are written alternately from the same in-memory world, so they protect against a torn write but not against a bad state. Suggest a third, daily snapshot slot.
- **The pre-profile save slot is written during onboarding.** The 30-second autosave runs before any profile exists, so `pocket-town:save:a/b` holds a starter town that the first player then inherits. Harmless today, but it means the "legacy town" path always fires for new devices.
- **Flaky e2e tests, now fixed:** "a short break does not show the postcard" and its sibling sometimes failed. It looked like timing, but the cause was that the tests tapped the middle floor spot while Pim, playing the music box right behind it, covered it; the tap went to Pim. The tests now pick a floor spot nobody is standing on.

## Checks after the changes

- `npm run lint`, `npm test` (130 tests, including new ones for the piano's gate, its tuning and the furniture style) and `npm run build` pass.
- Re-run in the browser at phone, tablet and desktop sizes: the first run, every room in every building, the map, the house wizard end to end, decorating and the piano. No errors and no third-party requests.
- The room reference pictures were re-made in the CI image (`npm run e2e:screenshots`), since the furniture changed how rooms look. Then the full e2e suite ran in the same image: 109 passed, 1 flaky (above), 16 skipped by design.
- Offline after the service worker installs: plays. No console errors, no CSP violations, no third-party requests.
